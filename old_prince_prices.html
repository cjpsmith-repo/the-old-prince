What the winners paid

Every lot that drew at least one bid and could be attributed to a player, across 767 finished games. Two questions: do the players who go on to win pay less for a given private, and does it matter which railway the private's share belongs to?

Question one

Winners pay less — barely

The gap between what eventual winners paid and what everyone else paid, per lot, with 95% confidence intervals. Sixteen of eighteen lots lean the same way, which is hard to dismiss. But almost every bar's whisker crosses zero: in dollar terms the discipline is worth two or three dollars a lot, not twenty.

Question two

The same price, a different railway

Eight of the eleven privates come with a share of whichever company is the Mainline or Shortline that game — and that company is drawn at random during setup. Each cell is the buyer's average finish when the attached share belonged to that railway. 0.500 is neutral; the small figure is what they paid.
