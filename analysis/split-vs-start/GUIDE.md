# Split, start, or neither: a decision guide for The Old Prince 1871

Use this on your stock turn whenever a tranche slot is open. Dollar figures are the average effect on your **final net worth**, measured across 796 replayed games on 18xx.games (average final net worth is about $4,700). The reasoning and the analysis behind each step are in `FINDINGS.md`.

## Step 1. Should you take the slot at all?

Take it, unless one of these is true:

| Situation | Value of taking the slot | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 7 or D | about $0 | Too few operating rounds left for the new company to turn its bank cash into dividends and share-price steps. Cash left in a company at game end counts for nobody. |
| Only one or two slots left on the whole chart | about $0 | These slots fill late, so the same time problem applies. |
| 3-player game | small, about +$40 | Six slots for three players. If you skip one, you'll likely get another chance. |
| 4-player game | large, about +$200 | Six slots for four players. If you skip, a rival usually takes it. |

The slot is worth most in phases 3+ and 4+, about +$200 to +$230, and when you're already ahead. If you pass, spend the action buying shares instead.

## Step 2. Which options can you actually take?

- **Split.** You must be president of the parent with at least 40%, and the parent must have at least two stations on the map. It costs no cash up front. Budget for about three branch shares at par, roughly $200, to float it this round.
- **Start a company.** You need twice par for the president's certificate and enough buyers to reach 60%. If you float it alone, that's about six shares at par, roughly $390 at a $65 par.

If only one is possible, take it, subject to Step 1. The exception: never split a parent that has no train.

## Step 3. If both are possible, should you split or start?

**First, three deal-breakers. If any applies, start instead of splitting.**
1. **The parent has no train.** About -$500. After the split both companies must own a train. The branch's money covers one, so the parent dumps its treasury shares or you pay from your own pocket.
2. **After the share exchange, an opponent would own as much of the parent as you.** You lose the parent later half the time. Your stake can shrink to the president's certificate, and the parent's treasury now holds shares anyone can buy.
3. **You can't float the branch this round.** About -$230. It earns nothing until it floats, and the parent has already given up its station.

**Then weigh these:**

| Leans toward splitting | Leans toward starting |
|---|---|
| The parent's non-president shares are **mostly in the market**: about +$140 | They're **mostly held by opponents**: about -$120. Opponents get branch shares free and end up owning about a quarter of your branch, which starts with less money. |
| The parent has **spare cash** to hand the branch: about +$40 per $100 | The parent's treasury is nearly empty |
| You can give a station in a city that **keeps an open slot**, or at the end of a line | The only station you could give is on the parent's main through-route and would **fill the city**, blocking the parent |
| You're short of cash, since a split costs nothing up front | You have cash, and starting leaves your existing holdings untouched |

**Ignore these. They made no difference:** the paper loss from swapping parent shares for cheaper branch shares, who holds the PEIR share of the company you'd start, and your exact percentage of the parent.

## Step 4. If you split, do it right

1. **Give exactly one station, in a city that will still have an open slot.** If the city fills, the parent can't run through it and loses about 8% of its revenue half the time. With an open slot, the loss is under 1%. Giving two stations doubles the damage.
2. **Give the branch cash.** About +$110. In phase 7 or later this is mandatory. The branch can start at $58 par at most, so it gets at most $464, less than the cheapest train left at $500.
3. **Move a train** if the parent has two or more.
4. **Buy the float shares** on your next turns in the same stock round.
5. **Run the two companies as one pool.** Companies can sell trains to each other for $1 up to the buyer's entire treasury, so you can shift cash and trains between them whenever one needs it.
